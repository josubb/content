Spousal sponsorship can move forward only when the financial record is complete and consistent, because even small gaps in income documentation can trigger delays. When the I-864 does not show sufficient support, the case often pauses until the sponsor’s finances are clearly proven or supplemented.

A spousal green card case can stall over money before USCIS ever reviews the relationship. One missing figure, the wrong household size, or an underfilled affidavit can trigger a request for evidence and push the case back weeks or months. The real issue is not a single filing fee; it is proving the sponsor can support the immigrant without creating a public charge problem.

The total cost of a spousal sponsorship is more than the USCIS filing fee: it can also include medical exams, biometrics, translations, and related affidavit-of-support paperwork. The key step is proving the sponsor meets the income threshold with Form I-864, or supplementing with assets, a joint sponsor, or I-864A when income falls short. Knowing how spousal immigration sponsorship costs and financial affidavits fit together helps prevent delays before the packet is filed.
