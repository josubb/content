Avoid the UK bird peanut mistake that risks chicks

Feeding peanuts incorrectly can expose garden birds to contamination and create choking risks for chicks. Use aflatoxin-tested kernels and select a feeder that prevents young birds from accessing whole peanuts during the breeding season.

A fresh sack of peanuts can be a valuable garden-bird food, but safety depends on freshness, feeder design and season. Choose plain, bird-grade, aflatoxin-tested peanuts; use whole kernels only in mesh feeders and offer kibbled peanuts on clean trays.

Are peanuts safe for garden birds in England?

Peanuts are safe supplementary food for many garden birds in England when they are unsalted, unflavoured, aflatoxin-tested and served correctly. Their fat and protein are particularly useful in cold weather, but whole kernels should never be scattered on lawns, tables or open trays.

Can young birds choke on whole peanuts?

Young birds can choke on whole peanuts or large pieces if adults carry them from an open feeder to a nest. A mesh peanut feeder lets adult birds remove only small fragments.

Which peanuts are unsafe for wild birds?

Unsafe peanuts include salted, roasted, flavoured, snack-grade, stale or mouldy nuts. Buy food labelled for wild birds, ideally with an aflatoxin-testing claim, batch code and best-before date.

Before buying, use a quick safety checklist rather than relying on price or a generic "peanuts" label. Choose bird-grade peanuts from an identifiable supplier, with a sealed pack, a best-before date and a batch or lot code that can be traced if there is a problem. The label should confirm that they are plain, with no salt, sugar, flavourings or oil: unsalted peanuts for birds are the appropriate choice.

Prefer aflatoxin-tested peanuts, then inspect the contents at home for dust, webbing, insects, clumps, visible mould or an unusually rancid smell. A clear origin statement and a recent packing date are useful extra checks, especially when buying a larger sack.
