Leaving dry mealworms for birds can endanger nestlings

garden bird food | dried mealworms | nesting season | bird feeder hygiene

Source: https://gardenbirdsguide.co.uk/leaving-dry-mealworms-for-birds-can-endanger-nestlings/
Mealworms can bring robins close to the garden, especially during the breeding season. However, dry mealworms may pose a serious dehydration risk to nestlings unless they are properly soaked before feeding.

Mealworms for birds can attract robins, tits and blackbirds when natural insects are scarce, but use them as a small extra alongside seeds, suet, fruit and fresh water.

Mealworms are a supplement, not a full diet

Mealworms are useful extra food, not a complete menu for wild birds: they provide insect protein, but British garden birds also need seeds, berries, natural insects and water across the year.

Birds most likely to eat mealworms

Robins, blackbirds, starlings, great tits and blue tits are regular visitors to a mealworm tray. Put the tray near a shrub or fence cover, but leave a clear view so birds can spot cats before landing.

Parent birds collect many kinds of prey because chicks need moisture and a broad range of nutrients. Think of mealworms as a sandwich filling, not the whole packed lunch.

For a typical English garden: put out mealworms beside seed, suet or fruit, not instead of them.
