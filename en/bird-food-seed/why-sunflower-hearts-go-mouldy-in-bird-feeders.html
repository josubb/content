Why Sunflower Hearts Go Mouldy in Bird Feeders

Sunflower hearts spoil quickly when moisture, warmth, and feeder residue create ideal conditions for mould growth. In damp weather, offer only a 1–3 day supply and clean feeders regularly to protect visiting birds.

Sunflower hearts can go mouldy in bird feeders because they are hulled, so damp reaches the soft kernel quickly in England’s wet weather. They spoil fastest when a large feeder stays partly full for days, particularly in sheltered, humid spots where birds are feeding lightly.

Are sunflower hearts right for English gardens?

Sunflower hearts suit English feeders, especially finches and tits.

Sunflower hearts are simply sunflower kernels with the outer husk removed; they are not a separate type of seed. With no shell to open, small garden birds can eat more of each mouthful and leave almost no husk litter below the feeder. Their naturally high oil content makes them a useful high-energy food, particularly during cold spells, breeding season and busy periods of garden bird feeding.

Finches and tits commonly take them from a tube feeder, while sparrows may use both tubes and trays. Because the soft kernel is exposed, however, hearts need drier handling than whole seeds.
