A renewal quote can alter cyber coverage in ways that are easy to overlook but costly to discover after an incident. Retroactive dates and prior acts provisions determine whether earlier events remain insured when the carrier changes.

When switching cyber insurers, choose prior acts wording that matches your firm's cyber history. Full prior acts can protect events before the switch. A stated retroactive date limits cover to later events. Cover from inception starts only with the new policy.

Switching insurers need not remove protection for past events. The new policy must keep the right claims-made dates and terms. Check the schedule for its retroactive date or prior acts wording. Disclose known incidents before you switch.

A retroactive date is the earliest date an insurer may consider a cyber event. Prior acts cover may include events before the new policy starts. The claim must still meet the policy terms.

A claims-made policy works like a postbox with a closing time. The insurer needs the claim or notice within its policy period. The event itself may have happened earlier.

A pending or prior date often relates to earlier claims, court cases, or notified matters. A known circumstances exclusion can remove cover for facts known before the new policy began.
