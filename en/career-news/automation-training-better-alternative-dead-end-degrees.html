Automation is reshaping manufacturing, creating demand for technicians who can operate, maintain, and troubleshoot advanced equipment. Lemoore College’s automation training demonstrates how hands-on technical skills can provide a direct path to high-value industrial careers.

Lemoore College’s Automation Investment Is a Career-Signal, Not Just a Campus Upgrade

ABC30 Fresno reports that Lemoore College has added new automation equipment intended to prepare students for high-demand manufacturing careers. That may sound like a routine education-facility announcement. It is more consequential than that.

For students deciding between a broad four-year degree and a shorter technical program, the equipment represents a shift in what employers increasingly need: workers who can operate, troubleshoot, program, maintain, and improve automated production systems. Manufacturing is not disappearing. The repetitive portions of many manufacturing roles are being redesigned around machines, sensors, software, robotics, and control systems.

That distinction matters for anyone trying to avoid a dead-end degree or a career path with weak local demand. A credential has value when it builds capabilities that employers can verify and use. Modern automation training can do that—provided the program teaches more than button-pushing on a single piece of equipment.

Why Automation Changes the Manufacturing Career Equation

The old picture of manufacturing work is incomplete. Some entry-level jobs based primarily on repetitive manual tasks remain vulnerable to automation, offshoring, and productivity improvements. Positions that require monitoring, diagnostics, setup, quality control, electromechanical repair, and process optimization are different. Automation often increases the need for workers who understand how the production line works when it does not work.

A factory cannot simply install automated equipment and walk away. Someone must interpret alarms, identify whether a fault comes from a sensor, motor, pneumatic component, programmable logic controller (PLC), software setting, material-flow issue, or safety interlock.

*The next advantage may belong to the workers who can make the machines think...*
