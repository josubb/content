A potential feta cheese shortage could quickly affect Spanish shoppers, restaurants, and food producers that rely on its distinctive flavor and texture. Supply constraints may raise prices, alter menus, and accelerate interest in local cheese alternatives.

A feta shortage is more than a missing supermarket item

The warning of a potential feta cheese shortage deserves attention from Spanish cheese buyers, delicatessen owners, restaurateurs and artisan producers. Feta is not simply a generic white, salty cheese: within the European Union, the name Feta is protected as a PDO (Protected Designation of Origin). Authentic feta must be made in specified areas of Greece, using sheep’s milk or a mixture of sheep’s and goat’s milk, with goat’s milk limited under the product specification.

That legal and agricultural link means supply cannot be expanded overnight by a dairy plant elsewhere in Europe. If Greek milk availability, flock health, feed costs, weather conditions, labour or logistics constrain production, the market cannot legitimately replace the lost volume with cow’s-milk white cheese and still call it feta. For consumers, this can mean higher prices and less choice. For food businesses, it exposes a common weakness: relying on a single imported PDO cheese as though it were an interchangeable commodity.

For Spain’s cheese community, the story is an invitation to examine sourcing resilience while giving local sheep’s- and goat’s-milk cheesemakers a more visible place on the counter and menu.

Why feta supply is unusually sensitive

PDO protection makes substitution legally and commercially complicated

The strength of feta is also what limits its flexibility. PDO protection safeguards traditional production, territorial identity and the value returned to Greek producers. It prevents misleading labels, but it also means that a factory in Spain cannot solve a Greek feta shortfall by producing “Spanish feta.”

A retailer may sell a brined white cheese made in Spain, France, Bulgaria or elsewhere, provided the product is accurately described. It should not be presented as feta, nor should a menu use “feta” as shorthand for any crumbled white cheese.
