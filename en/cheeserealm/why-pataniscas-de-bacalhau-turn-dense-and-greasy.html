Why Pataniscas de Bacalhau Turn Dense and Greasy

Portuguese cooking | salt cod | cod fritters | Iberian recipes

Source: https://cheeserealm.com/why-pataniscas-de-bacalhau-turn-dense-and-greasy/
A batter can look perfectly balanced and still produce fritters that are dense, oily, and disappointing. Pataniscas de bacalhau depend on a loose, slow-ribbon batter and stable frying heat to stay light inside and crisp outside.

Pataniscas de bacalhau turn dense and greasy when the batter is treated like a thick fritter mix: it should fall slowly from a spoon, not hold its shape. Properly desalting the cod and frying small portions in hot oil keeps the edges crisp while the centre stays tender.

Pataniscas de bacalhau are Portuguese cod fritters made with shredded salt cod in a loose batter of flour, egg, onion and parsley—not potato croquettes like pastéis de bacalhau. Desalt the cod for 24–48 hours, then fry spoonfuls at 170–175°C in batches for a moist centre and lightly crisp, golden edges.

Follow the process and plan the cod

Plan the cod soaking and use 250 g desalted cod, 120 g flour, 2 eggs, cold water, onion and parsley for about 12 small fritters.

Shop for cod that suits fritters

For a reliable batch of Portuguese cod fritters, use 250 g of desalted, shredded cod, 120 g flour, 2 eggs, 1 small very finely chopped onion, 2 tablespoons chopped parsley and about 150 ml cold water, added gradually. After desalting the salt cod by soaking it in cold water and poaching it briefly, let the fish cool before combining it with the vegetables. Whisk the flour, eggs and most of the water until smooth, then fold in the cod.

The bacalhau batter should be a loose fritter batter: it should fall in a slow ribbon, carrying visible fish strands, rather than sit in a mound on the spoon.
