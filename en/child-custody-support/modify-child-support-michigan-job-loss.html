Quick summary: what matters most when modifying child support in Michigan

- Major premise: A substantial and continuing change in income is required to modify a support order under Michigan law (MCL 552.517). This includes job loss, reduced hours, or income changes from self-employment. - Timing matters: File a motion promptly after the change; temporary emergency relief can limit arrears while the court considers the motion. - Evidence wins cases: Pay stubs, termination notices, tax returns, benefit letters, and clear accounting for self-employment income are essential. - Temporary vs permanent: Courts distinguish between temporary layoffs, short-term unemployment benefits, and permanent income changes, outcomes differ accordingly. - Calculation basics: The Michigan child support formula uses combined parental income and the number of children; examples help estimate likely orders.
