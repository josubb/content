A cyberattack can disrupt reservations, payments, and customer trust in minutes. Cyber insurance may help cafés recover from ransomware, data breaches, and operational downtime, but policy limits and exclusions vary widely.

A single cyber incident can stop bookings, freeze card payments, and leave guests unable to check in or pay a bill. For a hotel, restaurant, bar, or café, that can mean lost revenue in hours, not days. The real question is not whether cyber risk exists, but which cover is likely to respond when systems, customer data, or online reservations are hit.

Insurance for hospitality and restaurants can help cover costs after ransomware, payment card theft, data breaches and business interruption, but policies vary widely. The right cover depends on how bookings, payments and guest data are handled, turnover, and security controls. A side-by-side comparison of cover, exclusions and premiums is essential before buying.

Is cyber cover worth it for hospitality?

Cyber cover is usually worth a close look for hotels, restaurants, bars, and cafés that depend on digital bookings, payment cards, Wi-Fi, or customer records. A single breach can bring costs that run from a few thousand pounds to tens of thousands, and the recovery bill often lands before trading normalises.

The legal point is simple: under UK GDPR, a breach can trigger reporting duties within 72 hours. That clock starts quickly, and the Information Commissioner's Office expects a proper response, not guesswork. The ICO explains the breach reporting rule here.

First-party losses vs third-party claims

First-party cover pays the business's own costs. That can include incident response, forensic help, data restoration, ransom negotiation, and lost income during downtime.

Third-party liability covers claims from other people.
