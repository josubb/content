Need an extra $300–800 a month to make rent less stressful? Urban renters juggling school, full-time work, kids or job hunting can test two realistic side hustles, pet-sitting via apps and mapped dog-walking routes, while worrying about real earnings, fees, lease rules, building access and safety.

Pet sitting apps vs dog walking routes

Pet-sitting apps often pay more per booking but come with higher admin, travel, and commission costs. Mapped dog-walking routes pay less per walk but scale when clients cluster nearby and time is blocked efficiently.

Net pay is usually 20–35% lower than advertised once platform fees, taxes, travel and unpaid admin are subtracted. Use a simple model: advertised rate minus platform fees and expenses equals realistic net hourly pay.

Insurance, lease rules and municipal permits determine if a gig is safe or allowed in a building. Platforms may offer liability coverage, but coverage often excludes lease breaches or condo rules.

How much rent can this cover by city?

A few multi-night housesits can cover 10–30% of monthly rent in many US cities when modeled net. For example, covering 15% of a $3,000 rent requires around $450 net per month from pet gigs.
