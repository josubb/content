An EICR can reveal faults that put people, property, and compliance at risk. The quality of the inspection depends on the competence, registration, insurance, and testing experience of the person carrying it out.

An EICR can look simple on paper, yet the wrong person can leave a landlord, homeowner, or agent with a report that is weak, disputed, or useless when it matters most. In Swansea and across the UK, the real risk is not just a bad inspection, but hiring someone who cannot prove the right competence, training, or insurance to stand behind the result.

Who can carry out EICR UK? An EICR should be carried out by a qualified and competent electrician who is trained to inspect and test fixed electrical installations, ideally one registered with a recognised scheme and insured for inspection work. The key is not just being an electrician, but proving competence, experience, and the right accreditation for a valid, defensible report.

Who can legally sign off an EICR?

A valid EICR comes from someone who can inspect, test, and judge the condition of fixed wiring with confidence. In practice, that usually means a qualified electrician who has specific training in periodic inspection, not just a general installer.

The title matters less than the proof. An electrician might be excellent at fitting sockets, yet still be a poor choice for an EICR if they cannot explain why a defect is coded one way rather than another.

The key question is simple: who can do EICR October 2026 under the standards that matter now? The answer is anyone who can show real competence, proper registration where relevant, and enough experience to produce a report that stands up to challenge.

Competence beats job title

A job title does not make a report valid. A person can call themselves an electrician and still lack the inspection and testing skill needed for a defendable EICR.

That matters because an EICR is not a quick visual check.
