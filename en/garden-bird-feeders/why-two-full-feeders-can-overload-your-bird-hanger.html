Why two full feeders can overload your bird hanger

bird feeder hangers | bird feeding stations | garden bird feeding | squirrel baffles | wild bird hygiene

Source: https://gardenbirdsguide.co.uk/why-two-full-feeders-can-overload-your-bird-hanger/
Two full feeders can double the load on a hanger and turn a seemingly safe setup into a failure point. Weight capacity, fixing stability, and feeding clearance matter more than the number of available hooks.

A full bird feeder can strain a light hanger long before both hooks look busy. Choose support by the weight of filled feeders, their movement in wind and the strength of the fixing.

Choose by loaded weight, not hook count

Start with the complete loaded weight, including food, rainwater and every feeder.

A stated capacity is a ceiling, not a target. For a modest garden setup, allow roughly 1 to 2 kg per filled seed feeder, depending on size and food, and leave 25% to 50% spare capacity below the hanger’s stated limit for wet food and swinging loads.

Work out the full hanging load

Weigh filled feeders where possible, or add the feeder’s empty weight to its food capacity. Large squirrel-proof feeders and damp fat-ball feeders can be heavier than they look.

Check reach as well as height

Leave at least 20 to 30 cm between a feeder and a wall, fence or trunk, with more clearance for wide squirrel-proof models.

Choose metal made for weather

Galvanised steel and well-finished powder-coated metal resist weather better than untreated steel, but inspect chips, welds and threaded joints regularly.

Match the support to your garden space

Choose a support that suits both the available space and the surface carrying the load.

A healthy mature branch suits one light or medium feeder, while a wall bracket needs sound masonry and exterior-rated fixings. For several feeders, a firmly anchored pole or feeding station usually gives the safest flexibility; a tall station can still lean in soft, rain-soaked soil.
