Don't Put Your Whole $500 Into Amazon FBA Inventory

A $500 budget can validate an Amazon FBA product idea, but it can disappear quickly if every dollar is tied up in inventory. Reserve cash for fees, returns, shipping adjustments, and the reorder cycle before scaling.

FBA can be worth starting with $500 for small-scale retail or online arbitrage—not private label—if you put roughly $250–$300 into inventory, reserve the rest for shipping, Amazon fees, returns, and reorders, and focus on testing products rather than generating immediate income.

Is $500 enough for a cautious FBA start?

It can be enough to test, not to build a full brand.

Split the money before sourcing

A workable starter budget puts $250 to $300 into several small inventory tests, not one large purchase. Keep $50 to $100 for labels, poly bags, and inbound shipping to Amazon fulfillment centers. Hold another $50 to $100 for price drops, returns, or removal costs, then leave the remaining balance available for a reorder.

With $500, Amazon FBA works better as a side hustle than as a full-time income plan. The first 30 to 90 days are usually about learning product sourcing, ungating limits, prep requirements, and cash-flow timing while inventory is checked in and sold. A realistic target is not a salary; it is finding a few replenishable products that can be bought again at the same or better margin.

If a test produces $50 to $150 in contribution profit and returns most of the cash quickly, reinvesting that cash can build inventory over time. Full-time selling generally requires more working capital because reorders, returns, account costs, and slower-moving stock all compete for the same cash.

Where your $500 goes after fees

Sales revenue is not profit.

For a $100 item in a category with a 15% referral fee, Amazon takes $15 before the FBA fulfillment fee.
