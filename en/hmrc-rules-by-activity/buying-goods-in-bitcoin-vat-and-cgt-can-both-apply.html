Buying goods in Bitcoin? VAT and CGT can both apply

Source: https://mindyourownbusiness.uk/buying-goods-in-bitcoin-vat-and-cgt-can-both-apply/
Paying with Bitcoin can feel immediate and borderless, but the tax treatment is not simplified by the technology. In England, a purchase may attract 20% VAT while the BTC spent can also be treated as a disposal for Capital Gains Tax purposes.

Paying or accepting Bitcoin for a sale in England can trigger two tax issues at once: the seller may need to account for VAT, while the buyer may have made a Capital Gains Tax disposal. Miss the GBP value at the right moment and a £120 purchase could leave incorrect VAT records, a flawed invoice or an undeclared gain.

Using Bitcoin to buy goods does not remove VAT: the seller charges VAT on the goods in pounds and records the BTC received at its GBP value at the VAT tax point. The buyer may separately realise a CGT gain or loss when spending the Bitcoin. You need the calculation, supporting records, edge cases and the 14-day VAT invoice rule; confirm unusual or high-value transactions with a tax professional.

Bitcoin payment does not make goods VAT-free

A taxable sale remains taxable when Bitcoin is the payment method. If a shop sells a £100 item plus 20% VAT, the customer owes £120 worth of Bitcoin, based on the agreed GBP value and the relevant time.

VAT follows the supply of goods, meaning the item the customer receives. Think of Bitcoin as the method used to settle the bill, much like a card, cash, or bank transfer. It does not change a standard-rated, reduced-rated, or zero-rated item into something else.
