Uptime is not a binary metric: every additional “9” reduces allowable downtime while increasing infrastructure cost and operational complexity. The right choice depends on the financial impact of interruption, recovery objectives, and the guarantees your workload actually requires.

How much is one extra “9” worth? Moving from 99.9% uptime to 99.99% saves about 7.9 hours yearly. At $1,000 lost per hour that equals roughly $660 per month. At $1,500 per hour that equals roughly $990 per month. That gap often exceeds the price difference between a cheap VPS and a premium cloud for many SMB workloads.

SLA tradeoffs matter when budgets and uptime collide. Cheap VPS often cuts cost by 50–80% at purchase. Those plans usually claim about 99.9% availability and show variable I/O. Premium managed cloud costs more. Premium clouds commonly offer 99.95–99.99% uptime, formal RTO and RPO, automated failover, and support. Quantify downtime cost and compute cost per extra '9' before buying.
