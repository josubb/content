A fixed calendar date can leave late-migrating hummingbirds without a critical energy source. Feeder removal should follow local sightings, frost risk, and winter records rather than a single seasonal deadline.

The hummingbird feeder hangs quiet after a cool September morning. Remove it about two weeks after your last local hummingbird sighting, not on a fixed calendar date; leaving it up does not delay migration.

Use sightings and frost to pick your feeder date

The best removal date is usually 14 days after your last hummingbird sighting, unless local records show winter visitors. Check recent reports through the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, eBird, or a local Audubon chapter when a bird appears after your usual season.

Regional timing is only a starting point

Regional windows help you plan, but your own last sighting matters more than a national date, especially where elevation and coastal weather change conditions quickly.
