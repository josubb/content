Logging Outcomes, Not Decisions, Breaks Luck Tracking

luck tracking | decision journal | probability basics | opportunity recognition | personal analytics

Source: https://luckmethod.com/logging-outcomes-not-decisions-breaks-luck-tracking/
A short streak can feel conclusive, but it rarely contains enough evidence to distinguish sound decisions from random variance. Logging outcomes across 30 to 100 comparable events creates a more reliable basis for separating execution, chance, and perceived luck.

You send 20 job applications, take a few networking calls, and one unexpected referral lands an interview. It feels like luck—but was it? Without a record of the effort, opportunities, and odds behind each result, your brain will naturally spotlight the memorable win and ignore the quieter pattern.

Luck Analytics & Tracking can’t predict fortunate events, but it can show whether your results exceed what chance alone would produce. Track decisions, opportunities, expected odds, and outcomes over time, then compare what happened with a realistic baseline and uncertainty range.

Follow the process and create a usable log

Start with one area of life and use a repeatable system.

Build one event definition

Choose one event type for the next 30 days. Write a one-sentence definition in the first row of your sheet, such as: “One event equals a tailored application sent to a job that meets my pay and skill criteria.”

Use one event unit only. Do not place a casual networking chat, a job application, and a recruiter interview in the same dataset because each has a different base rate, or usual chance of success.
