Why private clinics' medical cover won't fix data breaches

private clinics | patient data | private medical insurance | cyber insurance | UK SMEs

Source: https://dealergen.uk/why-private-clinics-medical-cover-won-t-fix-data-breaches/
A data breach can expose a clinic’s most sensitive asset: patient trust. Medical cover may protect clinical activity, but it does not address the financial, legal, and operational consequences of compromised patient data.

When patient records are unavailable, incomplete or shared poorly, care, billing and trust can suffer. Medical insurance usually covers eligible treatment costs, not system loss, breach response or regulatory duties.

Private clinics: data & insurance gaps are separate risks for clinic owners in England.

Data gaps and funding gaps need different fixes

Clinical-data gaps affect safe care. Insurance gaps affect who pays.

Missing records can disrupt safe treatment

A missing record is a continuity-of-care issue. It can affect diagnosis, prescribing, consent, surgery plans or follow-up.

The risk is higher for patients with complex medicines, allergies or recent NHS treatment. A missing allergy list can change a safe treatment decision.

Funding gaps can create unexpected bills

Pre-authorisation means an insurer has agreed to consider specified eligible care. It is not a blank cheque for every part of treatment.

A scan, consultant, anaesthetist, implant, procedure or follow-up visit may have separate terms. Check each part before treatment begins.
