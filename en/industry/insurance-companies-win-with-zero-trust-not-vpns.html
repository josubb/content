Insurance Companies Win With Zero Trust, Not VPNs

Zero Trust | Insurance Security | NIST | Identity and Access Management | Third-Party Risk

Source: https://zerotrustexplained.com/insurance-companies-win-with-zero-trust-not-vpns/
Insurance operations depend on uninterrupted access to claims, underwriting, and policyholder data—making every exposed connection a potential ransomware entry point. Zero Trust replaces broad VPN access with continuous verification and least-privilege controls that protect critical workflows without slowing authorized teams.

A claims adjuster is trying to pull files from home. A broker needs access to a quote portal. A third-party administrator is waiting on policy data. The VPN stays up, but the blast radius stays wide, and one stolen credential can still open the door to systems that were never meant to be broadly reachable.

Zero Trust for insurance companies means verifying every user, device, and application continuously while limiting access to only what is needed. For insurers, the real value is reducing ransomware and cyber risk without breaking claims, underwriting, broker, or TPA workflows. The best approach is a phased roadmap with measurable ROI, legacy integration, and user-friction controls.

Decide where zero trust actually beats VPNs

For insurance companies, Zero Trust should replace VPN access only where identity, device posture, and app-level control can be checked before each session. Everywhere else, a hybrid model is safer in the short term, especially when claims, underwriting, or policy admin still depend on legacy paths.

The wrong comparison is Zero Trust versus VPN as if one must win everywhere. The real decision is which users, apps, and vendors can move now, and which ones need staged controls first.

*The next breach may not begin with a failed control, but with one connection trusted too long…*
