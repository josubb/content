Losing spouse-sponsored health coverage after divorce creates immediate financial and healthcare uncertainty. This brief compares COBRA continuation and Marketplace enrollment on costs, subsidy eligibility, and critical deadlines to guide prompt decision-making.

Lost spouse‑sponsored coverage right after separation?

Missing enrollment windows or picking costly COBRA without checking Marketplace subsidies can create a dangerous coverage gap.

Act fast because deadlines, paperwork, and surprise premiums can spike household costs.

Key variables to choose COBRA or marketplace after divorce

The main decision variables are cost after subsidy, deadlines, provider access, and upcoming medical needs.

These four factors usually decide whether COBRA or the Marketplace is cheaper and safer.

How to calculate net cost quickly

Gather these four items: COBRA monthly premium, Marketplace monthly premium, estimated APTC, and expected annual out-of-pocket (OOP).

Then compute annual nets for each option.

COBRA annual cost equals COBRA premium times 12 plus expected COBRA OOP.

Marketplace annual cost equals (Marketplace premium minus APTC) times 12 plus expected Marketplace OOP.

A citable rule: the COBRA election period is 60 days from the later of coverage loss or the date the election notice is provided.

This deadline is the pivot point for action.

Do state rules or mini‑COBRA change the math?

Many states add protections or extend durations beyond federal COBRA.

California, New York, and New Jersey have notable state changes that affect duration and notices.

Action step: check your state insurance department or your state Marketplace for mini‑COBRA rules and forms.

HealthCare.gov posts Marketplace SEP rules and APTC guidance.
