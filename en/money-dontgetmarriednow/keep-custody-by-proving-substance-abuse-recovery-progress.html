Courts judge substance use by current risk to the child and pattern of use. They focus on treatment compliance and verifiable evidence, not a single past incident.

Documented treatment and regular testing improve court outcomes. Professional evaluations and a court-ready reunification plan help judges decide.

Concrete checklists and templates align evidence and timelines with what judges accept.

Summary of the process

1. Gather verifiable evidence: certified toxicology, signed treatment plan, attendance logs. 2. Get an early mental health custody evaluation to document parenting capacity. 3. Start a court-ready reunification plan with timelines and measurable milestones. 4. Use supervised visitation reasons to request or accept monitored visits while proving progress. 5. Present an evidence packet and timeline to the judge at the next hearing.

Laws and court practices vary by state. Expect different timelines and evidentiary needs.

Some states focus on immediate child safety and allow quick emergency actions. Other states apply the family-law "best interest" standard and favor gradual reunification.
