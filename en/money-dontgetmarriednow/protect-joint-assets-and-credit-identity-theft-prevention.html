Divorce or prenup negotiations can leave you exposed to identity theft and sudden damage to credit and shared assets. Freeze credit, secure accounts, document any suspected fraud, and protect joint assets—Act now to safeguard credit.

Worried that separation, divorce, or a prenup negotiation could expose joint accounts to fraud? Identity theft can harm credit, retirement, and your bargaining position. Use the checklists and account-lockdown steps below to act now and preserve evidence.

Summary of the process

1. Freeze credit at the three bureaus to block new accounts. 2. Secure online accounts: change passwords and enable two-factor authentication. 3. File reports: IdentityTheft.gov and local police to begin formal recovery. 4. Notify banks, payroll, and the IRS if tax fraud is suspected. 5. Add prenup or separation clauses that allocate restoration costs and define procedures.

Step 1: secure credit and online accounts

The first action is freezing credit with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. A freeze blocks most creditors from opening new accounts that rely on the credit file.

How to place a credit freeze

Request a freeze online at each bureau or by phone. Online lifts are often immediate; phone or mail can take up to three business days.

Fraud alert and monitoring explained

A fraud alert asks creditors to verify identity before approving new accounts; it does not block new accounts.
