Why Rat-Proof Feeders Still Attract Rats in UK Gardens

Source: https://gardenbirdsguide.co.uk/why-rat-proof-feeders-still-attract-rats-in-uk-gardens/
Finding rats beneath a bird feeder is frustrating, especially when the feeder is marketed as rat-proof. In UK gardens, spilled seed, accumulated husks and nearby cover can still create an accessible food source.

A rat-proof bird feeder in the UK can cut down the food available to rats, especially when you choose a caged, weight-activated or protected hopper design. Position it in the open, use a seed catcher tray and keep it at least 2 metres from fences, walls, shrubs and other climbing routes.

Rat-proof means less food, not no rats

A rat-resistant feeder reduces access to bird food; it does not make rats disappear from an English garden.

Signs food is drawing rats

Fresh dark droppings, gnaw marks on plastic feeders, shallow holes near paving and disturbed mulch point to regular rat visits. Check just after dusk for several evenings, rather than judging from one sighting in daylight.

A rat-resistant feeder cannot solve an established infestation with active burrows, shelter and several food sources. The Royal Society for the Protection of Birds (RSPB) also advises good feeder hygiene and avoiding food build-up beneath feeding areas.

Choose a feeder by birds, risk and budget

For most gardens in England, a metal caged feeder or a weight-activated feeder is the best starting point, but only with a tray that you empty often.
