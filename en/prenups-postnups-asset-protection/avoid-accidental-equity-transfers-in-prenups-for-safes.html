SAFEs in Prenups: Prevent Accidental Equity Transfers

Source: https://money.dontgetmarriednow.com/avoid-accidental-equity-transfers-in-prenups-for-safes/
Founders often stake not just capital but their livelihoods and identities in a startup; marriage can unintentionally expose that stake. A prenuptial agreement must clearly define the treatment of SAFEs, convertible notes and equity options to prevent accidental transfers, forced buyouts, and dilution.

Who owns unconverted SAFEs, convertible notes, and unvested options after a founder divorces? Founders and investors often miss language that treats those instruments as marital assets. That oversight can cause unwanted dilution, forced buyouts, and enforcement gaps across states.

Prenuptial clauses for venture financings and investor protections can make the intent clear. They define which instruments remain separate property and how conversion and valuation work. They also set vesting rules, buyout triggers, and enforcement mechanics.

Prenup clauses for venture financings & investor protections

The prenup should say whether specific SAFEs, convertible notes, and options are separate property. This clarity cuts the chance of future disputes. Courts still examine tracing, commingling, valuation timing, and local family law when reviewing conversions.

Define a conversion event by date or corporate action. That keeps the math simple when a SAFE converts after marriage.

Tie valuation to a documented event or formula to avoid repeated litigation. A fixed method speeds settlement and helps preserve investor deal flow.

Pause to check each clause against company documents.

What instruments must be listed?

List each instrument class by name and identifier. For example: SAFE (YC Series), Convertible Note dated [mm/dd/yyyy], Option Grant ID [#].

Add the principal terms that matter for division. That includes purchase price, conversion cap, discount, and liquidation preference.

List warrants, RSUs, and restricted stock grants tied to milestones. Each instrument often needs a different divorce treatment.
