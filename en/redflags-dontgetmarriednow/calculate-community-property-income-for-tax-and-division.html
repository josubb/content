Accurate computation of community property income is essential to ensure correct tax liability and equitable division between spouses. Precise adjustments, clear allocations, and thorough documentation reduce audit risk and minimize future disputes.

Summary of the process: one-line roadmap and outcome

Follow five ordered steps to compute and document allocations ready for filing. Each step yields a ready-to-file schedule and an audit checklist. The output maps to Form 8958 and settlement exhibits.

Gather-and-file roadmap

1. Gather primary documents and set separation and valuation dates. 2. Classify income and trace sources monthly. 3. Compute line-by-line splits and build a supporting schedule. 4. Fill Form 8958 and attach the schedule when filing MFS. 5. Reconcile software outputs and state law differences before filing.

Follow each step exactly to withstand IRS review.

Step 1: gather documents and set the allocation window

Collect documents that show income dates and ownership. Include paystubs, payroll registers, brokerage statements, canceled checks, bank ledgers, employer pay dates, account opening records, and signed prenups or postnups. Expect assembly to take between 2 and 8 hours based on account count.

Which dates define the allocation window?

Use the legal separation date or the court valuation date to split income. For decedents use the date of death as the cut-off. The most common error here is using the calendar year rather than the actual separation or death date.

What immediate documents to assemble?

List each income type and attach one representative monthly statement for the year. For wages include payroll calendars and W-2s. For investments include trade confirmations, dividend activity, and 1099s.
