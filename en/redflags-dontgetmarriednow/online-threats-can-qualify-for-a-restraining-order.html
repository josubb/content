Online harassment can feel immediate, invasive, and difficult to escape, especially when it follows you across devices and accounts. When the conduct is persistent and documented, it can meet legal standards for protective relief.

A string of threatening texts, fake profiles, repeated DMs, or someone watching every post can feel private and hard to prove. But online abuse can move fast from annoying to dangerous, especially when an ex, neighbor, coworker, or stranger keeps showing up in digital spaces and making fear feel constant.

Yes—can you get a restraining order for online harassment? In many U.S. cases, yes. Online harassment can support a restraining order or order of protection when it includes threats, stalking, repeated unwanted contact, or conduct that causes a credible fear of harm. Screenshots, usernames, timestamps, messages, and preserved profiles can turn digital abuse into court-ready evidence, even when there is no family or dating relationship.

Can online harassment qualify for court protection?

Online harassment can qualify for court protection when it crosses from rude or upsetting into threatening, stalker-like, or fear-based conduct. A judge usually wants to see repeated conduct, a link to the respondent, and proof that the behavior made you feel unsafe.

When a judge may say yes

A judge may say yes when the respondent sends threats, follows your accounts, impersonates you, doxxes your address, or keeps contacting you after being told to stop.

*The key question is not whether the threat was digital, but whether the record proves a pattern strong enough to trigger court protection...*
