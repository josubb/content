UVB deficiency can develop silently, even when a reptile enclosure appears properly lit. Correct UVB exposure depends on UVI, lamp distance, mesh obstruction, and species-specific requirements—not wattage alone.

Choosing UVB by box percentage or lamp wattage can leave your reptile underexposed. This can happen even with a new bulb.

Distance, reflector quality and mesh can cut UVB at the basking area. The true problem is not the bulb's label. It is the UVB that reaches your animal.

UVB lighting for reptiles and tortoises helps many species make vitamin D3 for calcium use. Wattage alone cannot determine the right lamp.

Match the measured UV Index at your animal's position to its Ferguson Zone. Give shade, use a safe mounting distance and replace lamps on time.

Pick UVB by UVI, not the box percentage

UVI is the useful measure because it shows the UV level where your reptile actually sits.

Match the species to its Ferguson Zone

A bearded dragon, veiled chameleon and Hermann's tortoise often need higher basking UVI. A crested gecko, ball python or dart frog needs much less.

Mediterranean tortoises and day-basking lizards often suit Ferguson Zone 3 exposure. Many nocturnal, twilight-active and shade-dwelling animals need low Zone 1 or Zone 2 exposure.
