Hot sockets and flickering lights can indicate hidden electrical faults that should not be ignored. An EICR identifies circuit defects, deterioration, and safety risks to determine whether urgent repairs or a full rewire may be required.

Signs that need urgent electrical action

Some signs cannot wait.

Emergency: heat, smell or shocks

Heat, burning smells, visible charring, and shocks are emergency signs. They can point to a loose or damaged connection.

A poor connection is like a kink in a hose. Electricity meets resistance at one small point, which can create dangerous heat.

Stop using the affected socket, switch, or circuit when you notice heat, smoke, or a burning smell.

Call a qualified electrician promptly if you see black or brown marks around a socket. Also call if a switch feels warm, you hear arcing sounds, or you receive a mild shock.

Intermittent faults still count. A common error is dismissing a smell because it disappears after a few minutes.

Priority: repeated trips and flickering

Frequent circuit breaker tripping needs a professional inspection, especially when it happens during normal use.

Different protective devices trip for different reasons. An MCB may trip because of an overload or short circuit.

An RCD or RCBO disconnects power when current takes an unsafe path. Damaged insulation can cause this.

Do not keep resetting a device that repeatedly trips.

Note what was in use when it tripped, then ask an electrician to test the circuit.
