Don't replace an arc fault breaker with the wrong AFDD

arc fault breaker | AFDD protection | consumer units | electrical safety Swansea | BS 7671

Source: https://swansea-electrician.co.uk/don-t-replace-an-arc-fault-breaker-with-the-wrong-afdd/
Repeated breaker trips can signal a real electrical fault, not just an inconvenience. Before arranging a replacement, confirm whether the device is an AFDD, RCD, or RCBO to avoid fitting the wrong protection type.

Repeated tripping can mean an arc breaker is reacting to damaged wiring, a loose connection, or a faulty appliance. Do not keep resetting it. Isolate appliances, note any indicator lights, and arrange safe testing by a registered electrician.

Is this an AFCI trip, or an RCD or RCBO trip?

A tripped switch in a Swansea consumer unit is often an RCD, MCB, or RCBO. It may not be an AFDD. Check the front label before assuming an arc-fault issue.

An MCB trips on overloads or short circuits. An RCD responds to possible earth leakage. An RCBO combines both jobs. An AFDD detects arcing patterns linked to damaged or loose connections.

Check the front label only

Read the device writing with the consumer-unit cover closed. Labels may say MCB, RCD, RCBO, or AFDD. A circuit name may also appear, such as “kitchen” or “upstairs sockets”.

Take a clear photo for the electrician. Do not remove the cover to inspect wiring. Live parts can remain energised.

A test button is not a diagnosis

A test button shows that an RCD or RCBO can trip. It does not explain why it tripped during normal use. It also cannot prove an appliance or cable is safe.

If the device trips again straight after one reset, unplug portable loads first. Leave it off and arrange inspection. Do not reset it again.

Swansea homes use AFDDs, not imported AFCIs

An AFCI is mainly a North American arc fault circuit interrupter. An AFDD is the comparable device used in UK consumer units.

An imported AFCI breaker or AFCI outlet must not be adapted to a British consumer unit. It may look similar, but it is not made for that board.

UK protective devices must match the consumer-unit maker, busbar layout, voltage, rating, and fitting instructions. This helps them work properly during a fault.
