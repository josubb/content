Substrate depth is not a cosmetic setting; it directly affects a reptile’s ability to dig, regulate humidity, and avoid unsafe heat exposure. A single “standard” layer can create hidden risks when it does not match the species, size, and behavior of the animal.

Using one blanket reptile substrate can create a welfare problem: shallow layers deny digging species a retreat, while overly deep or wet layers can trap moisture or interfere with heating. Match depth to behaviour, enclosure design and safe heat delivery.

Choose usable depth by behaviour, not labels

The usable depth is the upper layer your animal can stand on, dig into or use, not the total depth visible through the glass.

How deep does my animal need?

Routine substrate is often shallower than an egg-laying area: a female bearded dragon may need a separate oviposition box with around 15–20 cm of suitable digging mix, even when her usual layer is 5–10 cm.
