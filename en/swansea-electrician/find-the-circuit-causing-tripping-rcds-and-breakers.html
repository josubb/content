When an RCD or breaker trips, the circuit is protecting you from a fault that may already be affecting safety and continuity. Rapid identification of the cause reduces downtime and limits the risk of recurring trips, equipment damage, or concealed electrical defects.

A circuit that keeps tripping can turn a normal day into a guessing game, especially when the fault comes and goes. In Swansea homes and small businesses, the cause is often hidden behind a single appliance, a damp socket, or a circuit that is carrying more than it should.

If tripping RCDs and breakers keep happening, the fault is usually a leaking appliance, an overloaded circuit, a short, or moisture in wiring. The safest way to narrow it down is to isolate appliances, reset circuits one by one, and look for patterns under load. Repeated trips, burning smells, or visible moisture need a qualified electrician.

What to do first after a trip

A single trip often points to one bad item, not a full house problem. The fastest safe move is to switch off the affected circuit, unplug obvious appliances, and then reset only once.

The whole check usually takes 10 to 20 minutes if the fault is simple. If the same device trips again straight away, stop there. That pattern matters.

Check which device actually tripped

Look at the consumer unit and identify the device that dropped. An RCD protects against leakage to earth, while an MCB protects against overload and short circuit.

That difference saves time. A lot of people reset the wrong device and chase the wrong cause, which is where the confusion starts.

Switch off the load first

Turn off lights, unplug kettles, heaters, washing machines, chargers, and anything with a motor or heating element. These are the usual suspects because they pull the most current when they start.

A common mistake is leaving everything plugged in and just pushing the switch up again. It may work once, then it trips again and looks random.

Compare the RCD and MCB symptoms

An RCD trip usually means current is leaking somewhere it should not.
