A persistent chirp from a mains smoke alarm can signal anything from a depleted backup battery to an incompatible replacement unit. Safe replacement depends on isolating the circuit, matching the alarm system and completing functional tests.

The alarm starts chirping at 2am. Its expiry date may have passed. After a refurbishment, you may also be unsure about the ceiling base.

Replace it yourself or call a Swansea electrician?

A confirmed like-for-like swap can be reasonable. However, any doubt about wires, compatibility, or linked alarms means an electrician should take over.

A replacement is only like-for-like when the maker, range, base, terminals, and interlink method match approved instructions. Check the model number on the old alarm and base. Do not rely on shape or the front label.

Use a qualified Swansea electrician if the base is cracked. Also call one if conductors are loose or labels differ from the manual.

A heat alarm electrician is the right choice for a kitchen heat detector. Heat detectors reduce false alarms from cooking fumes.

A matching shape does not prove a matching connection.

Check compatibility before buying a replacement

Interlinked smoke alarms need an approved alarm, base, and communication method. Otherwise, they may fail to activate together.

Match the base and product family

Check the exact maker range, not only the brand. An Aico alarm from one generation may need a different base from another Aico range.

Both units may look alike, but their connections can still differ.
