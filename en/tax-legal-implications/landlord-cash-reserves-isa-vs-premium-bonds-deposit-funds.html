Landlord cash reserves must remain accessible enough to cover urgent repairs, void periods, or tenant deposit liabilities without creating unnecessary tax drag. ISAs and Premium Bonds offer different balances of capital security, liquidity, and return potential for funds held outside the property itself.

¿Te preocupa having tenants' deposits readily available, protected and tax-efficient while avoiding legal risks? Many landlords face a trade-off between tax sheltering, access speed and predictability of returns. This guide provides clear, practical answers specific to Landlord Cash Reserves: ISA vs Premium Bonds for Deposit Funds.

Key takeaways: what to know in 1 minute

- ISAs protect interest from income tax, making them attractive for landlords who want predictable, tax-free income on deposit reserves. Cash ISAs are straightforward for short-term holding. - Premium Bonds offer prize-based returns, not interest; prizes are tax-free, but expected return is uncertain and access may be slower depending on holdings and NS&I withdrawal process. - Tenancy Deposit Protection (TDP) rules, access speed and clear accounting are decisive: funds used to secure protected deposits must be available at short notice and clearly documented. - Liquidity and legal risk matter more than small yield differences when reserves must cover deposit disputes or emergency repairs. Prioritise access and traceability. - Inheritance and estate considerations differ: ISAs transfer rules and Premium Bonds holdings have distinct probate and nomination procedures that affect heirs.

*The better reserve vehicle may depend less on headline returns than on the cost of needing the money tomorrow...*
