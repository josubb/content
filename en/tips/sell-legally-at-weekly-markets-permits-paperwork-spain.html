Selling at a weekly market in Spain requires meeting municipal, tax, and product-specific requirements before the first stall is opened. A clear permit strategy reduces administrative risk and avoids interruptions in trading.

To sell at a weekly market in Spain, the seller needs municipal authorization, tax registration and product permits. The seller also needs civil liability insurance and, sometimes, social security registration.

Summary of the process

This summary gives a fast, step-by-step map to start selling legally at a market.

1. Check the local Ayuntamiento rules and book a stall date. 2. Register activity with AEAT using Modelo 036/037 and prepare invoices. 3. Decide between occasional seller or alta as autónomo and register in TGSS if needed. 4. Attach product permits: food handlers, RGSEAA entries, alcohol licences where required. 5. Buy civil liability insurance and submit the municipal application with annexes.

Where to start

Begin at the local Ayuntamiento commerce office or ventanilla única for market regulations and application forms. The municipal office allocates stalls, fixes fees, and sets annex requirements.

What each step achieves

Municipal authorization gives the right to occupy the space. AEAT registration records tax duties and VAT obligations. Sanitary or alcohol permits allow sales of regulated products.

A common mistake at this stage is missing required annexes.

Step 1: apply at the ayuntamiento

Start at the Ayuntamiento because every market is licensed locally and rules differ by town. The municipal commerce office or market organiser decides fees, forms and allocation procedures.

Find the correct office

Search the local website for “mercados”, “comercio ambulante” or “ventanilla única”.

*The key detail is often hidden in the local ordinance, and that is where the real difference between approval and rejection begins...*
