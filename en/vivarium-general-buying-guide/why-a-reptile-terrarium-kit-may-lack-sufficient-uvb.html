Why a reptile terrarium kit may lack sufficient UVB

reptile vivariums | reptile enclosure sizes | reptile heating and UVB

Source: https://reptilevivarium.co.uk/why-a-reptile-terrarium-kit-may-lack-sufficient-uvb/
A 90–120 cm terrarium may provide adequate floor space for many adult reptiles, yet enclosure size alone does not ensure suitable husbandry. Kit-included UVB lighting can be underspecified, poorly positioned, or unable to deliver the required exposure through mesh and distance.

A starter kit looks reassuring on the shelf. It may include a glass enclosure, lamp dome, substrate and a small UVB unit.

Yet the UVB tube may be too short, too weak or badly placed for a bearded dragon. The enclosure may also lack room for a basking zone and a cool retreat.

A reptile terrarium is not one-size-fits-all. Match its size, material, vents, UVB fittings and heat layout to the species, adult size and behaviour.

Choose a reptile terrarium by adult behaviour

The right enclosure lets an animal move, control body heat and act as its species should.

Floor area and height serve different needs.

Floor space and height are not interchangeable

Ground-dwelling reptiles need usable floor space. A leopard gecko, tortoise or bearded dragon cannot replace ground space with extra height.

Climbing species need height and safe routes between branches. Crested geckos and chameleons use the upper parts of their enclosure much more than ground-dwelling species do.

The most common mistake is buying a tall tank for a ground species. It looks large, but it does not create a warm end and a cool end.

Buy for the adult animal, not the baby

Buy for the adult animal, even if the youngster looks tiny. A small enclosure soon becomes wasted money when the animal grows.

A young bearded dragon may fit in a small starter enclosure. An adult needs at least 120 × 60 × 60 cm for useful movement and lighting.

Think of the enclosure as a room, not a box. The animal needs separate places for heat, rest, food and shelter.

A vivarium is the whole managed habitat: enclosure, heat, light, ventilation, substrate, hides and water.
