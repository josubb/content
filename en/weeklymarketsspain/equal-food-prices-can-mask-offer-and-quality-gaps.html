Similar shelf prices can hide meaningful differences in quantity, freshness, origin, and promotional conditions. Comparing the unit price and product quality helps identify the offer that delivers real value for your weekly shop.

Saturday morning: tomatoes at the market and a supermarket punnet both show a similar price per kilo. Yet one was picked recently, comes from nearby and will last several days; the other may be smaller, less ripe or sold in a pack that changes the real cost. The visible price alone rarely tells the full story.

When it comes to Precios, ofertas y comparación de calidad, the best value is not always at the cheapest stall or supermarket. Compare the same product by price per kilo, freshness, origin, season, pack size and expected use-by date. A five-category basket—fresh food, staples, own-brand goods, gourmet items and household products—helps reveal when Spanish markets offer better quality and when supermarket promotions are genuinely cheaper.

Quick comparison: where each basket item pays off

A weekly market is usually strongest for seasonal fruit and vegetables, while supermarkets usually win for own-brand staples, cleaning products and baby items. A unit price means the cost per kilo, litre or single item, and it is the only price that lets two different packs compete fairly.
