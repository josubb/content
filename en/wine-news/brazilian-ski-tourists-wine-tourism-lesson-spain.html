Chile’s winter rebound is a strategic signal for Spanish wine tourism

Vinetur reports that Brazilian ski tourists are helping revive Chile’s winter wine tourism. The headline may concern the Andes, but the commercial lesson is highly relevant to Spain: wine regions do not need to depend exclusively on harvest-time visitors, nor should they market winter as a low-demand period to endure.

The key development is the combination of two motivations that are usually promoted separately: skiing and wine. Brazilian travellers fly south for snow, a rare and aspirational experience for a market with no domestic ski culture. Once in Chile, many have both the spending power and the available time to add winery visits, restaurant bookings, premium tastings and bottle purchases to their itineraries.

For Spanish producers, wine-route managers, hotels and specialist travel advisers, this is evidence that winter wine tourism works best when it is attached to a stronger reason to travel. A cellar-door visit alone may not persuade someone to take an international trip in January. But a well-designed itinerary combining skiing, gastronomy, landscape, wellness and wine can.

Why Brazilian visitors matter beyond Chile

Brazil is a sizeable long-haul travel market with a growing appetite for premium experiences. Its travellers are also accustomed to treating wine as part of a wider social and gastronomic occasion rather than a purely technical tasting exercise. That makes them attractive guests for Spanish regions able to offer hospitality, storytelling and food alongside a credible wine portfolio.

Chile has a structural advantage for Brazilian ski travellers: geographical proximity, direct air connections through Santiago and the immediate visual appeal of the Andes.
