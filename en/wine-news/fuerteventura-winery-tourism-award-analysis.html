A tourism award is more than a trophy for a winery

The news that a Fuerteventura winery has won a Canary Islands Tourism Award for Excellence deserves attention well beyond the island. It signals that wine is becoming a more visible part of Fuerteventura’s visitor economy—an important shift in a destination long associated primarily with beaches, wind sports and year-round sunshine.

For wine travellers, the award is a practical prompt: Fuerteventura should not be treated merely as a stop between larger Canary Islands wine regions. For wineries and hospitality professionals, it demonstrates that a vineyard can compete for tourism recognition when it offers a convincing sense of place, not simply a tasting counter and a retail shelf. And for the wider Spanish wine sector, it underlines a strategic reality: memorable wine tourism is increasingly built around landscape, interpretation, local food, accessibility and service, as much as around scores or grape varieties.

The reported recognition matters precisely because Fuerteventura is a challenging wine-growing location. Its dry climate, scarce rainfall, wind exposure and volcanic-influenced terrain create conditions very different from mainland Spanish regions. Those constraints may limit production, but they also give the island a distinctive story—provided that wineries communicate it honestly and turn it into a worthwhile visitor experience.
