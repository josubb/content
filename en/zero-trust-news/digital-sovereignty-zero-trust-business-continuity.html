Digital Sovereignty: A Zero Trust Continuity Priority

Digital sovereignty defines who controls critical data, identities, and operational dependencies when conditions become uncertain. Within a Zero Trust model, it strengthens continuity by reducing exposure to vendor lock-in, unauthorized access, and jurisdictional risk.

Digital Sovereignty Is No Longer Just a Compliance Conversation

ESET’s discussion of digital sovereignty and trust for business continuity points to a material change in how organizations need to assess cyber risk. For years, sovereignty was often treated as a narrow legal issue: where data is stored, which privacy laws apply, and whether a cloud provider has a regional data center. That approach is no longer adequate.

Digital sovereignty concerns an organization’s practical ability to control its data, identities, cryptographic keys, technology dependencies, and operational decisions—even during a cyberattack, geopolitical disruption, supplier failure, or government access request. It is not the same as keeping every workload in a local data center. An organization can use global cloud services while improving sovereignty, but only if it understands and actively governs who can access critical systems, under what conditions, and how it can continue operating when a dependency becomes unavailable.

For Zero Trust leaders, this makes sovereignty a continuity issue. Zero Trust is frequently reduced to multi-factor authentication or network segmentation. Those are important controls, but the broader model is based on continuously verifying access, applying least privilege, assuming breach, and limiting blast radius. The same principles are essential when the risk is not only an external attacker, but also excessive dependence on a single provider, opaque administrative access, or loss of control over encryption keys.

Why Trust Must Be Verifiable

Trust is unavoidable in modern IT. A company trusts cloud infrastructure providers, software vendors, managed service providers, identity platforms, certificate authorities, payment processors, and dozens of SaaS applications.

*The next continuity gap may not be a breach—it may be a dependency no one can control...*

The analysis in digital sovereignty a zero trust continuity puts this into broader context.
