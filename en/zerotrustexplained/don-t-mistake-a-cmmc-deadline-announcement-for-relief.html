Don't mistake a CMMC deadline announcement for relief

CMMC Reform Task Force | CMMC Phase 2 | DFARS 252.204-7021 | DFARS 252.204-7012 | SPRS reporting

Source: https://zerotrustexplained.com/don-t-mistake-a-cmmc-deadline-announcement-for-relief/
A CMMC deadline announcement may appear to create breathing room, but the next 60 days carry a separate compliance signal. The Task Force reporting milestone is not an automatic extension, waiver, or certification reprieve for contractors.

A CMMC deadline announcement can change planning assumptions. It may not change your current obligations.

If a prime, solicitation, or internal stakeholder treats the July 13, 2026 news as permission to pause work, risk remains. The immediate risk is contractual. DFARS clauses, SPRS requirements, and flowed-down terms may still apply. An authoritative action must change them.

The July 13, 2026 CMMC announcement may affect the Phase 2 rollout. It does not automatically erase CMMC Phase 1, DFARS clauses, SPRS reporting, or contract-specific flowdowns.

The Reform Task Force's 60-day report deadline is not a new certification deadline. Review your solicitation, award, modifications, and clause set before changing funding, assessment, or remediation priorities.

What the July announcement changes, and what it does not

The announcement changes only what an official DoD notice, rulemaking action, or contract modification says it changes. A pause or review does not itself alter FCI, CUI, or clauses in an award.

Does a phase pause erase DFARS duties?

No. A CMMC phased implementation decision is separate from DFARS 252.204-7012. That clause requires adequate security for covered defense information when it applies.

It also requires cyber incident reporting when the clause applies. It remains separate from commitments in a System Security Plan (SSP) or Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M).

Can a prime still request evidence?

Yes. A prime may request an SPRS score, SSP excerpts, POA&M status, or incident-response evidence. It may also request proof of assessment readiness.

The request needs support in its contract, subcontract flowdown, supplier terms, or proposal process.
