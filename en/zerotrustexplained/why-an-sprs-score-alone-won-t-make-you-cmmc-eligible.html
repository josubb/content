Why an SPRS score alone won’t make you CMMC eligible

CMMC Level 2 | SPRS score | NIST SP 800-171 | DoD contracts | CUI compliance

Source: https://zerotrustexplained.com/why-an-sprs-score-alone-won-t-make-you-cmmc-eligible/
An SPRS score can signal progress, but it does not establish CMMC eligibility on its own. Contract clauses, CUI scope, assessment requirements, and affirmation status determine whether a DoD opportunity is truly within reach.

SPRS is the DoD system where contractors report NIST SP 800-171 assessment results. It is not a CMMC certification. A Basic Assessment can score from -203 to 110. A conditional CMMC Level 2 result may need at least 88 points. It may also need an approved POA&M closed within 180 days. Your DFARS clauses, CUI scope, and solicitation CMMC designation control the requirement.

Does your contract require SPRS, CMMC, or both?

Your contract determines whether you need an SPRS self-assessment, a CMMC assessment, an annual affirmation, or several actions. Check the DFARS clauses, the data handled, and the acquisition's CMMC designation.

Read the clauses before scoring

DFARS 252.204-7020 gives the U.S. Department of Defense rights to assess the contractor. It also gives DoD access to relevant information. DFARS 252.204-7021 works with the acquisition's CMMC designation under 32 CFR Part 170. Together, they show whether CMMC applies and which level you need.

Match the requirement to the data

A system that stores, processes, or sends CUI may fall within Level 2 scope. CUI means Controlled Unclassified Information. It is sensitive government data that needs special safeguards.

A company handling only Federal Contract Information may face a Level 1 requirement. FCI includes contract terms and basic government business data. The prime's data-flow diagram matters more than an employee's guess about a folder.

The data path determines the scope.

Know what the prime can request

The most frequent error is treating a prime's email as the full legal requirement. Check the subcontract clause, exact CMMC level, and due date. A request for “CMMC compliance” is too vague.

*The score may open the record, but the contract decides the door…*
